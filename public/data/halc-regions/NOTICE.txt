Coverage and grouping / 范围与归属
The source reconstructs the Low Countries and adjacent northern France and
western Germany in 1500. Its bounds in WGS84 are 0.5394290755–8.3519884661 east
and 48.5402029861–54.0243305418 north. The Alps, Switzerland and Bavaria are
outside this layer. Source dates 1500-01-01 through 1500-12-31 identify the
reconstruction snapshot, not exact locality lifetimes.

14,145 of 14,863 source localities are grouped into 152 display/directory groups
using their complete, unchanged ADM1 string. Semicolon membership order is
preserved: for example AR;AM and AM;AR remain different groups. The 98 groups
whose ADM1 contains a semicolon preserve shared or contested affiliations.
Full ADM0 values, their constituent codes, fid membership, TYPE and QUALITY
counts are retained in groups-index.json.gz. These groups do not identify
single sovereign states or establish national boundaries.

718 source localities are excluded from this derived group layer: TYPE 8 natural
features and ADM1 memberships containing XX, including their overlap. XX means
small sovereign regions in the source and is never combined into one state.
All source localities remain in the separate original locality layer. QUALITY
describes the basis of reconstruction, including modern-derived and estimated
boundaries; it is not a numeric positional-accuracy rating.

Changes made for this atlas / 转换过程
Source WGS84 polygons are projected to Web Mercator and quantized independently
at z6–10 to the same global integer grid as the locality layer (MVT extent 8192,
buffer 64). Zero-tolerance collinear removal is checked for set equality.
The attached precision model is cleared without moving these coordinates.
Members of each complete ADM1 group are then unioned without a second snap.
Each resulting group is clipped to buffered tile bounds on the integer grid
and encoded in the halc_regions MVT source layer. New intersection nodes from
union can be fractional; clipping quantizes those nodes again. No geographic
smoothing, manual boundary repair, reconstructed date extension, membership
reassignment or modern-border replacement is performed.

The preserved canonical research GeoJSON is the inverse-projected exact union
of the z10-quantized members, without decimal rounding. Tiles are a separate
display conversion and are not claimed to be point-for-point identical to
that union or to a union of individually clipped locality tiles. Comparison
of three dense z10 tiles found 9, 19 and 18 grid-square units of set difference
in thin internal seams/holes; their exterior rings had zero measured Hausdorff
distance. This is a sample, not an all-tiles equality claim.

The separate halc_region_edges line layer removes coincident visible boundaries.
It unions all group boundary lines on each zoom's integer grid before clipping,
merges adjoining arcs, clips the resulting network, then unions on the integer
grid again to remove snapped coincidences. Group membership is unchanged. This
line-grid conversion can differ from query-polygon boundaries by quantization;
it is not a geographic smoothing process. The network does not include artificial
tile-frame edges. Draw this line layer uniformly and retain halc_regions polygons
for queries, rather than stroking each polygon and doubling coincident edges.

There are 814 tiles, including 449 valid empty tiles; all 152 groups appear at
each zoom z6–10. All tiles in the source's rectangular tile ranges are supplied.
At z10 one integer grid unit is about 4.78 Web-Mercator metres; ground distance
varies with latitude. Grid spacing and denser vectors do not establish the
historical accuracy of the reconstruction. Small parts can collapse during
quantization. Zooms beyond z10 enlarge the existing tiles without new detail.
The original GeoPackage is unchanged and can be downloaded from the source.
